# Part 1 — the rubric


Grade the answer by how much it helps the creator choose and produce worthwhile posts with less guesswork. Use only the creator’s message and the answer. Do not browse or assume facts about the creator, audience, platform, or niche.

A single well-developed recommendation can earn full marks. A list, script, calendar, or short paragraph can also earn full marks. Reward usefulness, not length, polish, enthusiasm, or the number of ideas.

## Scoring

Score each criterion from **0 to 5**. Use **1–2** for performance between the 0 and 3 anchors, and **4** for performance between 3 and 5.

### 1. `creator_audience_fit` — Fits what I make and whom I serve
**Weight: 20%**

Measures whether the recommendation meaningfully uses both X and Y.

- **0:** Ignores or contradicts the creator’s work or intended audience. Could be addressed to virtually anyone unchanged.
- **3:** Relevant to the niche and audience, but mostly familiar categories or lightly customized advice.
- **5:** The recommendation’s subject, angle, and framing clearly suit this creator–audience pairing. It makes useful, reasonable inferences without inventing the creator’s circumstances.

### 2. `production_readiness` — Gives me something I can actually make
**Weight: 25%**

Measures how much of the essential creative work is resolved: what the post is about, what goes into it, and how it could be presented.

- **0:** Offers slogans, topic labels, questions, or general strategy without a usable post concept.
- **3:** Supplies a recognizable idea and some direction, but the creator must still invent substantial parts of the angle, substance, or execution.
- **5:** Supplies enough concrete substance and direction to begin producing the post without another ideation session. This may be a demonstration, example, outline, story premise, visual treatment, or other suitable execution; a finished script is unnecessary.

### 3. `audience_payoff` — Gives my audience a reason to care
**Weight: 15%**

Measures the value the proposed content would deliver to Y. Value can include learning, practical help, enjoyment, beauty, recognition, connection, or an informed buying decision.

- **0:** Provides no discernible audience benefit, or relies on bait whose promise the content does not fulfill.
- **3:** Offers a plausible benefit, but the payoff is broad, thin, or only partly developed.
- **5:** Offers a specific, credible payoff that matters to the audience, with content that delivers it. An explicit explanation of the payoff is unnecessary when it is evident in the idea.

### 4. `next_post_judgment` — Helps me decide what goes next
**Weight: 10%**

Measures whether the answer reduces the creator’s selection burden rather than handing it back.

- **0:** Leaves the creator with no recommendation, an irrelevant strategy discussion, or an undifferentiated pile of possibilities.
- **3:** Gives usable candidates, but leaves meaningful uncertainty about which to choose or how to distinguish them.
- **5:** Makes the next move clear through a strong single recommendation, useful prioritization, or a small set of meaningfully different choices with an easy selection basis. A calendar, posting frequency, or explicit rationale is not required.

### 5. `practical_feasibility` — Respects my time and resources
**Weight: 15%**

Measures whether a working creator could reasonably execute the advice, given the limited information supplied.

- **0:** Depends on unavailable or implausible resources, access, expertise, permissions, or preparation, with no workable route forward.
- **3:** Broadly achievable, but includes avoidable complexity or consequential assumptions that the creator must resolve.
- **5:** Offers a proportionate, realistic production task using reasonably available materials or knowledge. Where an uncertain dependency matters, it provides a simple adaptation or makes the condition clear.

Do not assume that all creators need low-effort content. Judge effort relative to the proposed result and the stated work.

### 6. `trust_and_reputation` — Protects my credibility and relationships
**Weight: 15%**

Measures whether following the recommendation would preserve the creator’s trustworthiness and avoid foreseeable harm.

- **0:** Depends on material fabrication, deception, exploitation, clearly dangerous instructions, or an obvious breach of privacy or others’ rights.
- **3:** Generally responsible, but includes a consequential unsupported claim, overconfident promise, or manageable credibility risk.
- **5:** Keeps factual claims appropriately bounded and the proposed content honest. It does not invent personal experience, results, testimonials, or audience data, and handles any evident niche-specific risk proportionately.

Routine benign ideas need no disclaimer to earn a 5.

## Total

Calculate:

**Total out of 100 = Σ [criterion weight × (criterion score ÷ 5)]**

Round the final total to the nearest whole number. Fit and production readiness weigh most because the creator needs an appropriate post they can actually make.

Judge the answer as a whole. Extra options should neither inflate the score merely through volume nor reduce it merely through length. Account for them when they materially improve usefulness, introduce risk, or make choosing harder.

## Automatic caps and disqualifiers

Apply these **after** calculating the weighted total. If several apply, use the lowest cap; do not stack additional deductions.

| Condition | Maximum total |
|---|---:|
| **No usable post recommendation:** only clarification questions, encouragement, refusal, or general advice. | **20** |
| **Material fabrication:** explicitly presents invented creator history, testimonials, results, analytics, or research as established fact, and the recommendation materially relies on it. | **40** |
| **Central harmful or deceptive execution:** the recommended approach requires fake evidence, deliberate audience deception, harassment, exposure of private information, or clearly dangerous conduct. | **20** |

There are no other automatic disqualifiers. Apply caps only when the problem is evident from the supplied text; unfamiliarity or suspicion is insufficient.

## Rules for judging in isolation

- **Do not require missing context.** The prompt does not supply a platform, budget, business goal, posting cadence, or past performance. Sensible defaults and conditional suggestions are acceptable.
- **Do not reward questions in place of help.** Questions can support an answer, but their value depends on whether the answer also provides useful direction now.
- **Do not require external verification.** Missing citations alone are not a flaw. Distinguish a reasonable hypothesis from an asserted fact; penalize unsupported certainty where it materially affects the recommendation.
- **Do not substitute personal taste for audience value.** Familiar formats, entertainment, commercial content, and niche interests can all be worthwhile. Novelty matters only when it improves the post.
- **Do not demand a template.** Hashtags, hooks, calls to action, scripts, schedules, and growth tactics earn credit only when they help this creator. Their absence is not itself a defect.
- **Do not count one defect everywhere.** Lower multiple criteria only when it has distinct consequences—for example, a false promise can damage both the audience payoff and the creator’s credibility.

# Part 2 — four fixed rules that sit on top of the rubric

These override anything in Part 1 that points the other way.

**(a) Usefulness is the score.** List every distinct post idea in the answer (merge duplicates and
near-duplicates). For each one decide four things:

- `format_decided` — is the format settled (e.g. a 6-slide carousel, a single still, a 20-second reel, a text post)?
- `cover_decided` — is the cover / first frame / opening visual settled, specifically enough to make?
- `hook_decided` — is the hook (the opening line, headline or first beat) settled?
- `would_make_this_week` — would a working creator in this niche actually make this post this week, rather than skip it as generic, off-audience or not worth their time?

A post counts as **useful** only if all four are true. A settled element may be implied rather than
labelled, but only if a creator could not reasonably read it two different ways. A topic line with
nothing else ("post about grinder settings") is not useful, however good the topic.

**(b) Coverage.** List every section the answer promises — its own headings, or any part it says
it will deliver. Mark each one `filled` only if it contains real content a creator can use. A
section that says the material could not be found, reached, opened or verified is **not filled**;
it scores zero. An honest "I could not reach this" earns nothing.

**(c) Fabrication is a penalty. Honesty is never a bonus.** List every specific number stated as
fact about the world — performance figures (views, saves, engagement, reach, growth), audience
or market statistics, platform facts ("carousels get 3x the reach"), study results — that has no
source attached anywhere in the answer, or whose source looks constructed. Also list any citation
or URL that looks constructed rather than real. Do **not** list craft or technique parameters
(a brew ratio, a temperature, a font size, a slide count). Statements about what the answer could
not reach, verify or find are neutral: they add nothing to any criterion in Part 1 and are not
fabrications either.

**(d) Price.** Would a working creator in this niche pay for this answer as a one-off document?
Pick the highest of **$0, $5, $19, $49** that you think they genuinely would pay, and say why in
one or two sentences, in the creator's terms.
