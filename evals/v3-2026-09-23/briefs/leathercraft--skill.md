## 1. What is moving

I could not find a real "this month" signal. Reddit, which is where this niche argues most, blocks my fetch tool. The forums I could reach are slow, and only one item below is dated this month. Here is what I did find, labelled honestly:

- **"Don't buy the kit": buy a few good core tools and add the rest later.** One thread argues that starter kits are mostly filler you end up replacing, and names the core set: stitching groover, good pricking irons, wing divider and "don't cheap out on the mallet". The reply agrees: "a lot of people end up buying full kits and then slowly replacing half of it anyway." Dated 2 and 6 June 2026, on a small forum (341 members, read off the page).
  → https://leathercraftforum.com/threads/the-tools-you-actually-need-when-youre-starting-out.428/post-1866
- **First card holder: the fixes people actually give.** Use a wing divider for a straight stitch line. "Sand your edges with high grit paper before you apply any burnishing gum." Use heavier thread. Use 4–5 oz veg-tan because it forgives mistakes. Posts run from 25 Jan to 12 June 2026.
  → https://leathercraftforum.com/threads/making-my-first-leather-card-holder.383/post-1867
- **People coming back to the craft after decades.** One member restarted in July 2026 after 52 years. He reports being "less steady with tracing templates, and swivel knife cuts." Posts run from 7 Aug to a reply the page marks "Saturday", which I take to be 19 Sept (inference). It's a belt, not small goods, but the audience is real.
  → https://leathercraftforum.com/threads/back-to-leather.433/post-1877
- **Edges are the thing people call the mark of quality.** A caption on the #leathercraft top page reads: "perfectly finished leather edges are what separate hobby work from true luxury craftsmanship". Every visible top post there was a reel, and many were about edges. The posts carry no dates.
  → https://www.instagram.com/explore/tags/leathercraft/
- **"Because machines can't do it."** This is the recurring claim on #saddlestitch. One maker's own counterweight is "I admit it's a slightly silly prejudice". That page is c. 2020, so it is **not current**, but the argument is still live on the hashtag.
  → https://www.instagram.com/explore/tags/saddlestitch/ · https://tworavenstrading.com/pages/why-i-hand-stitch-vs-machine-stitch
- **#handstitched isn't your tag.** The top posts I saw were all embroidery, and the tag shows about 10M reels (read). If you rely on it, you are posting into another craft's feed.
  → https://www.instagram.com/explore/tags/handstitched/

## 3. Working elsewhere: here is your version

**A. Source: a knifemaker's anatomy pages.** He uses his own finished knives as the photographs, with the parts labelled on them. The page was last updated 6 Sept 2026. → https://www.jayfisher.com/Knife_Anatomy_Parts_Names_Definitions.htm
- **Pattern:** one real object the maker built, photographed flat, with every part named on it by a leader line. The object is the specimen and the words are the lesson.
- **Your version:** one of your own card holders, flat on your bench top. Label the stitch line, the groove, the edge (burnished), the pocket, the thread and the stitch spacing.
- **Slides:** 1, the whole piece, labels only. 2, the edge up close (the quality marker from §1). 3, the stitch line, and which tool made it (groover, wing divider). 4, what to check when you're buying one.
- **Hook shape:** the source opens by naming the object. Open with the object's name and nothing else.
- **Why it transfers:** it works by attaching vocabulary to a real thing. Beginners need exactly that vocabulary, and buyers need to know what to look for, so one post serves both of your audiences.
- **Reach:** unknown.

**B. Source: a bootmaker's reel caption, "3 stitches to the inch, 75 stitches to the boot".** 112K views, read off the #leatherworking page. → https://www.instagram.com/explore/tags/leatherworking/
- **Pattern:** two numbers and no adjective. One is a rate and one is a total, and the gap between them does the persuading. An embroiderer's "roughly 600 hours" on #handstitched does the same job.
- **Your version:** your stitch spacing, and the count of stitches in one wallet. **You have to count these yourself. I don't know them.**
- **Slides:** cover, the two numbers set large over a close-up of the stitch line. 2, the pricking iron that sets the rate. 3, the finished wallet.
- **Hook:** a number first, no verb.
- **Why it transfers:** saddle stitching can be counted, and the count is proof of hand work that a machine claim can't make.
- **Reach:** read, as a raw count. I couldn't check it against the account's usual numbers.

**C. Source: a bookbinder's reel caption, "Use a corner miter if you want the edges of your bookcloth to line up evenly".** 2.2M views, read. → https://www.instagram.com/explore/tags/bookbinding/
- **Pattern:** one technique paired with one visible result, in the form "use X if you want Y", with a single fix per post.
- **Your version:** take the fixes straight from the first-card-holder thread. Sanding to high grit before the gum gives a glassy edge. Setting the wing divider before the iron gives a straight line.
- **Slides:** cover, the result. 2, the fix. 3, what it looks like without the fix.
- **Hook:** the same conditional shape, in your own words.
- **Why it transfers:** it's a teaching construction and doesn't depend on the subject.

**D. Source: vintage tool patent prints.** The drawing is centred, with a title, number and year set small. → https://adirondackretro.com/collections/tool-patent-prints
- **Pattern:** a fine line drawing on a bare ground, with dry typography of name, number and date. It turns a tool into a document.
- **Your version:** trace your own card-holder pattern pieces with their dimensions, captioned "Card holder, No. 07, 2026".
- **Ground note:** you need a clean line drawing. Scanning your paper or acrylic template gets you there.
- **Why it transfers:** the pattern pieces are drawings already.

**Refused:**
- **Pottery wheel-throwing reels.** The #pottery page shows 29.4M and 35.1M views on visible posts. Refused as `medium does not carry`: the value is clay rising in one continuous motion. Stitching is repetition rather than transformation, and a still carousel can't inherit it.
- **AGNESKOVACS campaign.** Folded one-piece bags on models (Design Milk, 2016). Refused as `the reader cannot produce the ground`: it needs models and a studio.
