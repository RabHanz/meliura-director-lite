# What to post next: a content brief for a hand-stitched leather goods creator

## The short answer

**Next Instagram post:** a 9-slide carousel called **"Straight stitches on the front, slanted on the back? Here's why."** It's one of the most-repeated beginner questions on the leather forums. It's easy to save and to send to a friend, and anyone thinking of buying sees how closely you check your own stitching.

**Next Pinterest move, starting this week:** post gift Pins. People on Pinterest plan the holidays in September, and Pinterest's own guidance is to plan content 45–60 days ahead. Anything aimed at Christmas needs to go up between now and mid-October.

**Then:** a 6-week schedule (below) that switches between teaching beginners and showing buyers the craft. It ends with a clear holiday order deadline worked back from USPS's December 17 cutoff.

## 1. What's changed on the platforms, and what it means for you

I've included only the changes that affect what you should make.

**Instagram**
- **DM shares ("sends") are now the strongest signal for reaching people who don't follow you.** Instagram says sends and shares are among its most important ranking signals. Third-party write-ups put a send at roughly 3–5× a like, but that multiplier is their estimate, not Instagram's. *What it means for you:* make posts a beginner would send to someone they learn with. Teaching carousels do this better than finished-product shots.
- **On Reels, how much of the video people watch, and whether they rewatch it, matters more than how many start it.** A short loop watched to the end beats a longer video people drop out of. *What it means for you:* short, sound-on stitching clips of about 15–25 seconds with no talking.
- **Trial Reels** show a Reel only to non-followers first. If it does well within 72 hours, it can be shared to your followers automatically. It needs a professional account with at least 200 followers, and trials can now be scheduled. *What it means for you:* test hooks here without cluttering your grid.
- **Only 5 hashtags now count** (since December 2025). Anything past five is ignored.
- **Public posts from professional accounts are indexed by Google and Bing** (since July 10, 2025). Captions work as search text, so write "how to saddle stitch leather evenly," not just "🧵✨."
- **Original work is favoured.** In April 2026 the penalty for reposting other people's content was extended to photos and carousels. This helps you, since everything you post is your own bench. Don't post with a TikTok watermark.
- **The profile grid is now 3:4.** Stills at 1080×1440 aren't cropped on the grid. 4:5 images lose about 7% at top and bottom.
- **Adding music to a carousel** reportedly makes it eligible for the Reels feed. Most sources say 5–10 slides get the best saves and swipe-through.

**Pinterest**
- Pins are 2:3 (1000×1500). Video Pins are 9:16. All accounts can post video now, and old Idea Pins were turned into standard video Pins.
- Pinterest reads Pin titles, descriptions, board names and any text on the image. Keep board names short and keyword-led, with 20–100 Pins per board.
- New Pins get priority over repins, and posting steadily beats posting a lot at once.
- **Timing:** gift searches traditionally start in September. Self-gifting peaks in October and November, and gifts for others peak in December. Pinterest says to plan 45–60 days ahead.

## 2. Your two audiences, and posts that serve both

Beginners want **fixes and permission to make mistakes**. Buyers want **proof, a reason for the price, and a gift idea**. The most useful posts do both at once:

- A beginner carousel about a stitching flaw shows a buyer you care about the back of the stitching too.
- A buyer-facing cost breakdown shows a beginner what the work is really worth.
- A patina comparison helps a buyer decide and teaches a beginner about veg-tan leather.

On most posts, add one line in the caption for the other audience. For a teaching post: *"Every piece I sell gets checked on both faces before it ships."* For a sales post: *"Learning? The edge steps are in my highlights."*

## 3. The next post in detail

### Carousel: "Straight on the front, slanted on the back?"

**Why this topic:** Leatherworker.net has several separate threads on it: [slanted stitches only on the back](https://leatherworker.net/forum/topic/93695-getting-slanted-stitches-only-on-back-side/), [back of the stitch comes out different on every project](https://leatherworker.net/forum/topic/54618-backside-of-saddle-stitch-comes-out-different-on-every-project/), [zig-zag only on one side](https://leatherworker.net/forum/topic/56676-zig-zag-only-on-one-side/) and [the "slant" to saddle stitching](https://leatherworker.net/forum/topic/83008-the-slant-to-saddle-stitching/). A problem that keeps coming back and has no single agreed fix is ideal for a carousel. It's also something you can shoot in a single afternoon.

**Slides** (1080×1440; mostly macro shots with short text):
1. **Hook:** front and back of the same seam side by side. Text: *"Front looks right. Back looks wrong. Why?"*
2. **Why it happens:** diamond chisels cut angled holes. The thread lies neatly only if it settles into the same corner of the hole every time. A pointed awl or diamond iron is angled so the flat faces sit closest together, which keeps the leather between holes stronger (point from the forum).
3. **Cause 1: an inconsistent order.** Which needle goes through first, and which side of the other thread it passes, has to be identical on every stitch. Show your own sequence close up.
4. **Cause 2: pull direction.** Pull along the angle of the hole. One forum suggestion: left hand away from you and about 45° up, right hand down at the same angle. Show both hands.
5. **Cause 3: uneven tension.** Pull with the same gentle force every time. Pulling harder doesn't make it stronger; over-tight thread cuts into the leather ([Leather Beast](https://www.leatherbeast.com/blogpage/4-mistakes-to-avoid-when-saddle-stitching)).
6. **Cause 4: the holes themselves.** If you punch from one side only, the back holes aren't the same shape. Punching from both sides and thinner thread (around 0.35mm) give a more even result on both faces.
7. **An honest note:** on thin leather (about 1.5mm) the two faces may never match exactly. Decide which face people will see and make that one right. There isn't one correct method; you want to be consistent.
8. **Your own before and after,** ideally from your first months next to now.
9. **Call to action:** *"Send this to the person you learned with. Save it for your next seam."*

**Caption (sketch):**
> How to fix saddle stitches that slant on one side only — the #1 question I got when I started. It's almost always consistency: same needle first, same pull angle, same tension, every hole. Slide 7 is the honest bit. (Every piece I sell gets checked on both faces before it ships.)
> #leathercraft #saddlestitch #handstitched #leatherworkingbeginner #veganleatheralternatives ← swap the last one for #vegtanleather

Limit yourself to 5 hashtags. Add alt text describing the seam, and put a quiet acoustic track on the carousel.

**Cut the same footage into a Reel:** about 20 seconds of one slow stitch, sound on, macro, no voice, ending on the same frame it started on so it loops. Post it as a **Trial Reel** with the on-screen text *"why is only the back slanted?"* and see whether non-followers watch to the end.

**Pinterest version:** a 2:3 still titled *"Why Saddle Stitches Slant on One Side (and How to Fix It)"* on a board called **Leather Stitching Tips**, plus the Reel as a video Pin.

## 5. How to shoot and repurpose

- **One session gives you three posts:** macro stills for the carousel, a 9:16 video for the Reel and the Pinterest video, and a tall composite for a Pin.
- **Keep the same bench, light and background colour** from post to post so your work is recognisable in a feed of mass-produced wallets. Instagram's "originality" push rewards this.
- **Put text on stills for Pinterest, but little or none on Instagram stills.** Pinterest reads the words on the image; Instagram carousels only need a line of text per slide.
- **Leave a real mistake in now and then** (a burnished edge that went fuzzy, a thread you pierced). Beginners trust it, and it gives them something to comment on.

## 6. What to track, and what to do about it

- **Carousels:** look at sends and saves per reach, not likes. If the slant carousel's sends are well above your usual, make "fix-it" carousels your regular weekly format.
- **Trial Reels:** average watch time and replays. If non-followers watch the stitching loop to the end, try a new hook every week (a different flaw or tool each time).
- **Pinterest:** saves and outbound clicks, checked around 30 days in, since Pins take a while to build. Make more Pins like the best performers and let the weak ones sit.
- **Sales:** add a line like "found me on Pinterest?" to your order form. The Pinterest gift window is the easiest place to see whether posting is turning into orders.

## Caveats

- Several figures (carousel engagement rates, the 3–5× value of a send) come from social-media tool blogs, not from Instagram. The direction is consistent across sources, but treat the exact numbers loosely.
- Reddit blocks my fetch tool, so the evidence on beginner questions comes from Leatherworker.net, Tandy and supplier blogs.
- I haven't seen your account, prices or past analytics. Replace the placeholders in the cost-breakdown and order-deadline posts with your real numbers.
