## 1. Make this first

**"This week's set, drawn as a topo."**

**Why this one first:** Across gyms that log climbs through ClimbLife, 37% of a boulder's ascents happen in its first week and 20% in its second [S1]. Members act on a set in the week it goes up. You can make this with one phone photo and a line tool. The evidence and what you can make this week agree, so nothing had to be traded off.

- **Format:** four-slide carousel.
- **Cover:** one straight-on photo of the freshly set wall, taken before it gets busy, with the contrast turned down. Draw each new boulder as a numbered dashed line from its start hold to its top, using the guidebook topo technique (§2, first entry). Put nothing else on the frame except the zone's name, small.
- **Slide 2:** the key. For each number: hold colour, grade or circuit, and the setter's first name. One line each, the way a guidebook lists problems.
- **Slide 3:** one boulder from the set, in one line from its setter, saying why someone who climbs twice a week should try it this week.
- **Slide 4:** which zone closes next and when, from your gym's setting schedule. Movement publishes its schedule every two weeks so members know which zones will be shut [S14]. Your gym's dates go here.
- **Hook:** in the guidebook's shape, a count and then a place: *"___ new problems. Here's where they start."* The number is the setters' actual count for this set.
- **Caption job:** ask members which number they flashed. "Flash" is the niche's own word [S2].
- **What would make it fail:** a busy photo. If the lines have to fight chalk, people and bright holds, the topo reads as noise. Drop the photo's contrast until the lines are the only sharp thing on it.

## 2. It works in another niche. Here is your version.

### A. The guidebook topo (outdoor climbing guidebooks → the indoor set)
**Source:** Rock+Run, "Making Rock Climbing Topos" by <creator> [S20], dated 12 Nov 2018, opened 24 Sep 2026. **Basis:** pattern exists. The page is a how-to and says nothing about performance.

- **The pattern:** a photograph used only as ground, with the route traced over it as a curved line (the page says "set to solid or dashed is the most effective"). Each line carries a number that matches a text list, and "different line colors and styles distinguish between different features" [S20].
- **Your version:** see §1. One tip for a gym: make every line the same white dashed line and carry each boulder's colour only in its number badge. That keeps the photo from turning into a second set of competing colours.
- **Slides:** cover: wall and lines. 2: the key. 3: the setter's line. 4: next closure.
- **Hook:** the source leads with numbers and places, so yours does too: *"___ new problems. Here's where they start."*
- **Why it transfers:** a topo is a map of lines on a wall, and your wall is a wall. The only thing that changes is that the lines are new every week.
- **Leave alone:** the scale bar and north arrow the page recommends [S20]. Nobody needs to navigate your gym.

### B. The bookshop shelf talker → "Setter's pick"
**Source:** Publishers Weekly, *Shelf Talker* blog, <creator> [S15], dated 10 Feb 2020, opened 24 Sep 2026. Also <creator>, "Shelf talker" [S16], dated 28 May 2026. **Basis:** pattern exists. Both pages describe the practice. Neither gives numbers.

- **The pattern:** a small handwritten card in the staff member's own voice, kept short ("6 lines, 6 words per line seems to be the recipe") [S15]. Some shops give each bookseller "their own signature colored paper, so that customers can skip across sections looking for 'their' staffer's picks" [S15]. It works because it is one person's enthusiasm and not the shop's [S16].
- **Your version:** one static post per set. A setter writes two lines by hand on a card about the boulder they are proudest of, and you scan it. Give each setter one colour that doesn't change from week to week, so members learn whose picks they trust. Behind the card, a close crop of the boulder's start holds.
- **Hook:** first person with no build-up, in the shelf talker's shape: *"I set this for people who hate slopers."* Swap in the setter's real reason.
- **Why it transfers:** a setter is a named person with taste, choosing one thing out of a wall of choices. That is exactly a bookseller in front of a shelf. The setting trade already talks about setters as authors [S9].
- **Ground note:** it needs actual handwriting from the setters. A typed version loses the thing that makes it work.

### C. The bird field guide plate → "Which one is the start?"
**Source:** Wikipedia, "Field guide" [S18] and "Peterson Field Guides" [S19], opened 24 Sep 2026. **Basis:** publisher reports use. The page says Peterson's 1934 method "revolutionized" field guides. That describes influence and gives no outcome.

- **The pattern:** "paintings of similar species together, and marked with arrows showing the differences" [S18]. The system picks out "readily noticed visual features rather than… the technical features of interest to scientists" [S19]. Two things that look alike sit on one ground, with one arrow each at the one feature that tells them apart.
- **Your version:** for people who have never climbed. Put two holds that look the same to a newcomer side by side on one plain ground. Examples: a start hold with its tag and the same colour hold without one, or a sloper next to a jug. One arrow and one short label each, in the niche's own hold words (crimp, sloper, pinch, undercling [S2]).
- **Slides:** cover: the pair and the question. 2: the answer, with the arrow. 3: a second pair (where the top is and how you know you've finished). 4: "Come in on ___ and we'll show you the rest," with your intro session's day in the blank.
- **Hook:** a question the viewer can answer by looking: *"Which one's the start?"*
- **Why it transfers:** first-timers say "I don't know what I'm doing" [S3] and ask "Where do I go?" [S5]. A plate that teaches one visible difference at a time answers that without a wall of text.

### D. The ceramics kiln diary → "What didn't make the set"
**Source:** Socialmon, "63 pottery studio post ideas" [S21], dated 30 Mar 2026, opened 24 Sep 2026. **Basis:** publisher reports use. The page says kiln posts "naturally drive profile return visits and saves" but prints no numbers.

- **The pattern:** "what went in, what came out, any surprises," with the failures left in on purpose: "This piece cracked in the kiln. Here is why that happens" [S21].
- **Your version:** at the end of setting day, a two-slide post. The cover is a boulder the setters built and then stripped during forerunning, photographed before it came down. Slide 2 is one line from the setter on why it went. Movement describes setters forerunning and then "swap[ping] out holds" [S14]. That step is your kiln.
- **Hook:** the source leads with the failure, so yours does too: *"This one never made it to the wall."*
- **Why it transfers:** setting has a built-in pass or fail step that members never see, just like a kiln.
- **Ground note:** someone has to take one photo before the holds come off. Ask the head setter to make that part of the routine.

### Refused
**"Setting for Spectators"**, <creator>, CBJ [S11]. It says boulders for an audience "must look impossible," with movement that is "flashy, quick, powerful." **Refused for stills and carousels: `medium does not carry`.** The value is in watching the dyno happen, and a still of someone mid-dyno is a sports photo with no build-up. It would carry to a reel you film yourselves, so keep it for those.

## 4. What is moving

- **How long a boulder stays relevant.** ClimbLife says 37% of ascents come in week one and 20% in week two [S1], dated 6 Jul 2026. One gym moved from 6-week to 4-week rotation "to modify direction and address pain points much quicker" [S2], dated 28 Aug 2025. That second source is a year old.
- **Who the set is really for.** "Boulders up to V3 are about 42% of what gets set but 64% of what gets climbed" [S1], 6 Jul 2026. That's an argument about setting that members feel. Note that ClimbLife doesn't say how many gyms or logs this comes from.
- **Unfinished projects.** "Only about 1 in 10" is finished on a later visit, and the median gap between sessions is 7 days [S1], 6 Jul 2026.
- **The fears of first-timers, named out loud.** Movement's blog has four headings: "Everyone is going to judge me." / "I'm not strong enough." / "I don't know what I'm doing." / "I won't fit in." [S3], dated 28 Jul 2026. Rock Spot has "Where do I go? What do I need? What if I do something wrong? What if everyone is watching?" [S5], URL-dated 30 Apr 2026.
- **Setting as authorship.** The CBJ podcast "Writing and Setting Are the Same Job" with <creator> [S9], dated 10 Sep 2026. Central Rock Gym's "Route Setting Secrets" [S10], dated 1 Apr 2026, calls setting "puzzle design, physical intuition, and art."
- **Gyms under money pressure, focusing on existing members.** "Focus on the Customers You Already Have" is point 1 of 6 in CBJ [S7], dated 4 Sep 2026. It quotes an operator: social media doesn't drive sales directly, "but it builds reputation."
- **The one climbers' forum I could read had little indoor bouldering this week.** The UK Bouldering home page [S8], opened 24 Sep 2026, showed recent threads on knee pads, harnesses and topo ownership.
