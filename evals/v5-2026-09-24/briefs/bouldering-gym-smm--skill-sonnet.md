## 1. Make this first: the set report

**Why this one first:** every gym I opened posts new sets as a photo and "FRESH SET" [S5, S2]. A fixed-fields report is a different construction, and it repeats weekly. <creator> suggests roughly one post a week for a local gym [S3]. It needs only counts the setters already have.
The two pulled apart on evidence. The most-used pattern in the niche is the first-timer walkthrough, which all three beginner guides I opened do in long-form text [S8, S9, S26]. Makeability and differentiation decided it, not performance data, because I found none.

**Format:** three-slide carousel, posted on the same weekday after every reset.

**Cover:** a report card with four fields in the same order every week. The source report has four parts: 24-hour total, season total, base depth, and terrain open or closed [S19]. Mapped to a gym:
- New this reset, as a count, set largest.
- On the walls now, as a count.
- Set so far this year, as a count.
- Walls open or stripped, and the next reset day.

The setters supply every number. If the gym can't count a field honestly, drop that field for good rather than filling it some weeks.

**Slide 2:** the one new problem a first-timer can walk up to, with its start holds photographed and the gym's own circuit colour. Ask the setters which one. Beginner guides tell newcomers to "climb what looks fun" over chasing colours [S26], so don't attach a grade.

**Slide 3:** the problem the setters would put their name to, with the tag as the gym writes it.

**Hook:** the source leads with the number, so lead with the count. The blank is the setters' own new-problem count: *"___ new."*

**Caption job:** ask members which new problem has already thrown them off. That puts falling into a members' post where first-timers can read it.

**What would make it fail:** changing fields between weeks. The source stresses that fixed timing and fields are what let readers compare one day with the next [S19].

## 2. It works in another niche. Here is your version.

**A. Conditions report** (this is the §1 pick)
- **Source:** OpenSnow's explainer on the 24-hour snow report [S19], dated 27 Nov 2023, opened 24 Sep 2026.
- **Basis:** publisher reports use. The page says resorts send the same fields daily. It says nothing about performance as a post.
- **Pattern:** a few fixed fields in a fixed order, measured at a fixed time. The sameness is what makes the report comparable.
- **Why it transfers:** nothing in it depends on snow. A reset also produces countable, comparable numbers.

**B. Museum label → the problem label**
- **Source:** STQRY's museum-labels guide [S17], dated 28 Aug 2025, opened 24 Sep 2026.
- **Basis:** pattern exists. The guide describes a label's standard contents and says nothing about performance.
- **Pattern:** a made object framed by a stack of facts in a fixed order: maker, title in bold or italics, date, materials, dimensions. It runs to about 50 words for a simple object [S17]. The label makes the object read as authored.
- **Your version:** one problem from this week's set.
  - Title is the problem's name. Problem names are a valued tradition [S14, thread date unconfirmed]. If the gym doesn't name problems, ask a setter to name one.
  - Maker is the setter, and date is the day it went up.
  - "Medium" is hold types and wall angle in the setter's words.
  - Add one line from the setter on what the problem is about. Setting is being described as authorship this month [S6].
- **Slides:** cover is the label alone on a flat ground. 2 is the whole problem, photographed. 3 is the setter's line about the crux. 4 is the tag, where it is, and how long it stays up.
- **Hook:** title first, then maker: *"___ (name). Set by ___, ___ (date)."* All three blanks are the gym's own.
- **Leave alone:** accession number, credit line and provenance. Those are museum jokes that only land if your register is dry, and I can't read it.
- **Why it transfers:** a boulder problem has a maker, a date and materials, which is what a label is for.

**C. Dictionary entry → a word a first-timer hasn't got yet**
- **Source:** Dictionary.com Word of the Day [S20], dated 23 Sep 2026, opened 24 Sep 2026.
- **Basis:** pattern exists.
- **Pattern:** the headword carries the frame alone. It is followed, in fixed order, by a bracketed pronunciation, part of speech, a one-line definition, a line of context, and an example sentence.
- **Your version:** one gym word per card, taken from vocabulary I saw on gym pages: *beta*, *crimp*, *jugs*, *slab*, *overhang*, *dynamic* [S10], and *fall zone* [S9, S23]. Start with *beta*. The definition is in plain words. The example sentence is one a staff member has actually overheard. Don't let me or the model invent it.
- **Slides:** 1 word alone. 2 pronunciation and definition. 3 the example sentence. 4 the intro session's day and time.
- **Hook:** the word alone, with no explanation until slide 2: *"beta (n.)"*
- **Why it transfers:** unfamiliar vocabulary is part of what a first-timer meets. That is my reading. The pages say the barrier is uncertainty, not strength [S9].

**D. Run club schedule → two ways in**
- **Source:** Phoenix Run Club's schedule [S27], undated, opened 24 Sep 2026.
- **Basis:** pattern exists.
- **Pattern:** one recurring event with two options side by side ("A 3 or 5 mile option") and one welcome line ("There is a pace for everyone"). No run carries a "beginner" label.
- **Your version:** pick one session the gym already runs, and confirm which with the gym. Show two entry options at equal size, in the gym's own words (for example a circuit colour to start on, and a set to project). A first-timer and a regular read the same card, and neither is labelled.
- **Slides:** cover is the two options. 2 is option one on the wall. 3 is option two. 4 is day, time, how to book, and what to bring (rental shoes and a waiver at check-in are mentioned on another gym's page [S9]).
- **Hook:** the source's "3 or 5" has no verb: *"___ or ___."* Both blanks are the gym's own.
- **Caveat:** the source is a schedule page and says nothing about images. What carries over is the field structure.
- **Why it transfers:** the gym already serves both audiences on one floor.

**Refused:** Dogpatch Boulders' regrade of a whole gym [S1]. `the reader cannot produce the ground`. The page says it re-ranked over 300 problems from 11,000+ logged ascents by 650 climbers (Nov 2022–Feb 2023). Ask whether the gym logs ascents in an app and whether the owner will share the spread of grades. If so, a two-slide "spread inside one grade" post becomes possible. If not, leave it.
I found no page whose value was motion, so I offer no `medium does not carry` refusal.

## 3. Also worth making (ranked)

**2. Same tag, different problems** (members)
- **Format:** three-slide carousel.
- **Cover:** two problems from this week's set with the same tag as the gym writes it. Use the gym's own colour tag or grade, and don't add V-grades if the gym doesn't publish them.
- **Slide 2:** the first problem, plus the setter's one line on why it feels as it does.
- **Slide 3:** the second problem, same treatment.
- **Setter's menu:** wall style, hold type, movement sequence, the climber's strengths, and the setter's judgement that day. These are the factors a head setter's grading post gives [S10]. The setter picks one per problem.
- **Hook:** two problems, one tag, opposite feel: *"Same tag. Different night."*
- **Why:** the argument recurs across my sources [S1 2023, S10 2025, S26 2026]. The pages I opened answer it in text, not as a picture pair. That is my reading.
- **Fails if:** it becomes a defence of the grade. The line should be about movement.

**3. You will fall** (first-timers)
- **Format:** four-slide carousel.
- **Cover:** falling stated as the default.
- **Slide 2:** the floor, using the gym's own facts. Don't reuse another gym's wall height [S8].
- **Slide 3:** one line from staff on how to fall.
- **Slide 4:** the intro session's day and time.
- **Hook:** state the fear, then flip it: *"You will fall. Here's the floor."*
- **Why:** two gyms open with fear and answer in long blog posts [S8, S9].
- **Fails if:** safety claims aren't checked by your staff.

## 4. What is moving

- **Same grade, different problem, and vague colour circuits.** A regrade found "the hardest problem at a given grade… two or three grades harder than the easiest" [S1, Feb 2023, older]. A setter explains subjectivity [S10, 6 May 2025]. A gym tells newcomers not to fuss over "red versus purple" [S26, 3 Apr 2026].
- **Comp-style problems: is this climbing?** Some members find them closer to parkour. One gym's move to comp-style needed eight months of member feedback [S13, 9 Jun 2026].
- **Setting as authorship.** "Competition setting feels more like journalism and commercial setting feels more like fiction writing" [S6, 11 Sep 2026].
- **First-timer fear is uncertainty.** "Climbing usually feels intimidating before it feels exciting" [S9, undated, URL dated 30 Apr 2026]. "Falling isn't a failure in bouldering. It's built into the sport" [S8, 14 Jul 2026].
- **Retention over growth.** Trade press says to focus on existing customers, build beginner pathways, and protect routesetting quality [S7, 4 Sep 2026]. It questions whether social content sells. A 27-climber study found connection to other climbers the greatest facilitator, and overcrowding a concern for newer climbers [S11, 28 Feb 2025].
- **New sets are the default gym post.** New problems every Tuesday and Thursday, in the gym's own claim [S5, 18 Mar 2026]. 20 new routes plus comp dates [S2, 21 Oct 2025].
- **New holds.** Rock-scan textures converted to plastic, dual textures, and kids' families [S25, 14 Sep 2026]. Usable only if your gym bought them, so ask.
- **Elite scene, adjacent.** Bosi and Ghisolfi on Silence; Garnbret's 51st career gold in Koper [S12, editions 8 and 22 Sep 2026]. Comp viewers shape what members expect on the walls [S13].

**Whose conversation this is:** trade press and gym blogs. None of it comes from climbers talking among themselves, because Reddit blocked me. There is no Google Trends data.
