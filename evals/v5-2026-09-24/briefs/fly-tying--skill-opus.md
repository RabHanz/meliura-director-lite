# Brief: tying traditional wet flies and nymphs at the kitchen table, step by step, for beginners

*Assumed, because you didn't say:* the goal is to teach anglers who want to start tying. Carousels go on Instagram and each set gets a single-image version for Pinterest. You have a vise, your kitchen table, your own flies and your materials. You have about one evening per post. Depth: standard. All pages were opened on 24 Sep 2026.

**Your taste:** I haven't seen any of your saves or screenshots, so this brief has no reading of your taste. Every taste line below says "no saves seen." Paste 5–10 covers you like and I'll redo the ordering.

## 1. Make this first: "Three hackles"

**Why this one first:** Global FlyFisher's wet-fly hackle page gives turn counts: "For a full feather 2-4 turns will normally do". It says spiders often need one or two, with one side stripped. That page has **no photos comparing right and wrong** [S21]. Your audience is beginners, and this post fills that gap with things already on your table. I chose it because you can make it this week and it fills a gap on the page itself. It was not chosen because it's a hot topic: I found no evidence either way that hackle density is being argued about this month (see §4).

- **Format:** five-slide carousel, plus the cover on its own as a Pinterest pin.
- **Cover:** three Partridge & Orange flies, identical except for hackle turns: sparse, standard, overdressed. Same hook size, same silk, same camera position, same light, side by side. The only label on each is its turn count.
- **Slides:**
  - Slide 2 is the sparse one alone, close up, so you can count individual fibres.
  - Slide 3 is the overdressed one, close up at the head, showing the eye crowding. GFF: "aim for as small a head as possible" [S23].
  - Slide 4 is one step photo of stripping one side of the partridge feather [S21].
  - Slide 5 is the recipe card for the version you'd fish.
- **Hook:** bare counts, no verb: *"___ turns. ___ turns. ___ turns."* Fill the blanks with the counts you actually tied. Don't borrow them from this brief.
- **Caption job:** ask followers which of the three they'd tie on.
- **What would make it fail:** any change in angle, light or background between the three flies. The comparison only works if the hackle is the only thing that changes. <creator>'s first step-by-step rule is "fix the camera!" [S12].

## 2. It works in another niche. Here is your version.

**A. The sourdough crumb diagnosis.** It's the source of §1. → The Fresh Loaf, "Good, underproofed, and overproofed crumb examples" [S19], undated. Also Pantry Mama's troubleshooting guide [S18].
- **How it did there:** we only know the pattern exists. The Fresh Loaf thread shows 10+ comments, a count I read off the page, and there's nothing to compare it with.
- **The pattern:** the same object in three states, shot with the same framing. Each state gets a bare label, and one line says what you can see. Pantry Mama writes each fault as problem → cause → fix.
- **Your version:** one pattern in three hackle densities (§1). Or a Problem → Cause → Fix series: "bearded head" [S23], "crowded eye", "hackle that won't sit back". Each gets one photo of the fault on your own fly.
- **Why it transfers:** a diagnosis needs one variable changed with everything else held still, and a wet fly on a vise can do that.
- **Taste:** no saves seen.

**B. Knolling.** → Communication Arts, "The Flat-Lay on Knolling" [S15], undated.
- **How it did there:** the publisher reports use. The page says it can be "absorbed in one take, which is ideal for platforms where the audience might be scrolling through the feed very quickly." That is the page's claim, not a measurement.
- **The pattern:** objects "squared off to each other, with equal spacing between them", like placed next to like, shot "straight down from above".
- **Your version:** the cover of every step-by-step is the recipe knolled on the kitchen table, laid out left to right in tying order: hook, silk, rib, dubbing, the partridge feather. Everything is at right angles, with equal gaps and no labels. Slide 2 is the recipe in words.
- **Why it transfers:** a fly recipe is already a short, ordered list of physical objects.
- **Taste:** no saves seen.

**C. The herbarium sheet.** → Herbaria United wiki, "How to document a herbarium sheet" [S16], undated.
- **How it did there:** we only know the pattern exists.
- **The pattern:** one specimen centred on a plain sheet. A small label sits in a fixed corner, always carrying the same fields: name, collector, date, locality.
- **Your version:** one finished fly centred on plain card. The label in the lower right carries pattern name, original dresser, hook size, date tied and the water it's for. Use the same card and the same label position every time, so the set works as a Pinterest board.
- **Why it transfers:** traditional wets come with names, originators and history, and a specimen label holds all of that without a caption.
- **Taste:** no saves seen.

**D. "One year of knitting."** → <creator>, Just Good Shit [S17], 17 Jan 2022.
- **How it did there:** we only know the pattern exists.
- **The pattern:** early work shown beside later work, with in-progress photos kept in, alongside plain lessons such as "Knitting slower and reading the patterns slower would have solved like 75% of my knitting problems."
- **Your version:** your first Partridge & Orange next to this week's, shot at the same angle. Then one slide per thing you'd tell a beginner.
- **Hook shape:** a candid admission.
- **Ground note:** this only works if you kept your early flies. If you didn't, drop it.
- **Why it transfers:** your audience is where you started.

**Refused: the sourdough crumb shot as a single reveal** [S19]. Reason: `pattern inseparable from subject`. What it offers is cutting the loaf open to show the inside. Once the bread is gone, the matched framing is what's left, and A already uses that. A wet fly is judged from its outside profile, so nothing remains to be revealed.

**Not tested:** calligraphy "shadow exemplars", where one letter is repeated in fading grey. Both pages that described them failed to open, so I haven't used them.

## 3. Also worth making

1. **The knolled recipe (B) for a modern nymph.** Global FlyFisher's One Ribbon Nymph (18 Jun 2026) is tied with "cream artificial silk ribbon coloured with permanent marker pens" [S5]. It's cheap, and a beginner can get it from a craft shop.
   - Cover: the knolled materials.
   - Slides 2–6: steps, shot from one fixed camera position.
   - Hook: *"A ribbon and a marker pen."*
2. **Natural vs synthetic, wet.** <creator> compared a Pheasant Tail with an Organza Ribbon Nymph and noted they "look different when they are wet" [S6].
   - Cover: your two versions side by side, dry.
   - Slide 2: the same two flies in a glass of water.
   - Hook: *"Which is which?"* That's his opening line [S6]. Credit him if you reuse it.
3. **Specimen card series (C).** One traditional wet a week, same card, same label corner.
   - Start with the Partridge & Orange from §1 so the posts link to each other.
