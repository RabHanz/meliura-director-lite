## 1. Make this first

**Same tag, different problem.** A four-slide carousel, and a working title only.

**Why this one first:**
- **Fit with your members.** The latest CWA climber survey (8 Apr 2026) says nearly 75% of indoor climbers climb twice a week or more. Your members are that group.
- **What stalls them.** A CBJ-reported study of 27 climbers in Australia and Canada (28 Feb 2025) found new climbers stall, don't know how to keep improving, and quit. It also found that mentorship from gym staff was critical.
- **The grade argument.** Hörst's analysis of 40 US gyms (3 Dec 2025) says "a solid V4 climber could reasonably expect to climb V2–V6 across different gyms". A head setter's piece (Boulder Movement, 6 May 2025) opens on this exact contradiction.
- **Cost to make.** Two photographs and two short notes from your head setter.
- **What decided it.** The setter's words are the one thing only this gym can supply. The evidence is about climbers and gyms in general. I found no thread, count or comment from your own followers.

**Ask the gym for:**
- Two problems on the wall now that carry the same grade tag.
- The pair should differ in wall angle or hold type. Boulder Movement's own list is slab, overhang, vertical, dynamic, jugs versus crimps.
- The head setter's sign-off, because the post talks about the gym's grading in public.

**Cover:** the shared tag is the only type, set between two equal-size photographs so neither problem is favoured. The tag must be the gym's own. I have not put one in.

**Slides:**
- **2:** Problem A, with the setter's note on what it asks of you. Use the setter's own word for the movement. Hörst's category words are compression, crimps, slopers, dynamic and technical.
- **3:** Problem B, the same way, and what differs.
- **4:** the setter's one instruction on which to try next if the first felt harder, where each sits on the floor, and the gym's own date for when they come down.

**Hook:** take the shape of Boulder Movement's opener: one label, two different experiences. Don't take its words.

**Caption job:** ask which one they found harder. This post solicits beta. That matters because ASCEND's etiquette guide lists unsolicited beta ("spraying beta") as a social don't.

**What would make it fail:**
- It reads as defending your grades. Hörst's whole point is that grades vary between gyms, so keep the subject as style.
- The two problems are too alike.
- A setter's note runs past one idea.

## 2. It works in another niche. Here is your version.

*These are constructions, not performance evidence. I make no reach claim for any source in this section.*

### A. A bookshop's shelf-talker becomes the setter's note
**Source:** Gramercy Books, "Shelf talkers: find it here", page dated 21 Dec 2017, opened 23 Sep 2026. → https://gramercybooksbexley.com/blog/shelf-talkers-find-it-here

**The pattern:**
- A short first-person note, in the hand of the person who chose the thing, sits at the thing.
- It gives the personal reason and one specific detail.
- The page's examples are emotional reactions, such as "I sat down and read it in like two hours and then just sat for another two hours."
- MuseumNext's label advice (26 Dec 2024) adds the limits: one key idea, a hook first, and 50–100 words. → https://www.museumnext.com/article/10-tips-for-writing-effective-museum-exhibit-labels/

**Your version:**
- One card per problem, written by the setter, saying what they built it to make you do and where they expect people to go wrong.
- The frame is the photograph of the problem plus the note itself set large. A flat photo of the actual card is best.
- **My addition, not in either source:** give each setter their own card stock so followers learn whose problems they like.

**Slides:**
- **Cover:** the problem, with the note's first line.
- **2:** the crux, in the setter's words.
- **3:** the mistake they expect.
- **4:** where it is and when it comes down (the gym's own date).

**Hook:** the shape is a first-person reaction or intention, with no number. The words are the setter's.

**Why it transfers:** the reason sits next to the object in a named person's voice. <creator>'s setter podcast description (CBJ, 11 Sep 2026) says commercial setting "feels more like fiction writing", so setters are authors. → <creator’s page>

**Ground:** you need setters willing to write on card. If not, set it in type and say so on the slide.

### B. parkrun's First Timers Welcome becomes "who's on the floor for your first session"
**Source:** parkrun resources page, undated, opened 23 Sep 2026. → https://resources.parkrun.com/resources/volunteer-roles-explained-first-timers-welcome

**The pattern:**
- A named role whose whole job is the newcomer.
- Its brief has three parts: what this is, the ethos (who it's for), and one detail specific to this course.
- The page says seeing the role on the roster "may be very comforting to a brand new participant". The reassurance is structural: someone's job is you, before you arrive.

**Your version:**
- Name the staff member who meets first-timers and the hours they are on the floor. Those are the gym's own facts.
- Then the three parts: what bouldering is here, who it's for, and how problems are marked on this floor. Send Edition lists colour-coded holds and tape as its own beginner heading.
- Then one fear answered. Melanin Base Camp (July 2024) names three: being judged when you fall, the cost of shoes, and having no friend already climbing. → https://www.melaninbasecamp.com/trip-reports/2024/7/7/new-to-climbing

**Slides:**
- **Cover:** the person's name and their hours.
- **2:** what they'll say first.
- **3:** how problems are marked here.
- **4:** the one fear, answered in the gym's own words.

**Hook:** the shape is a person and a time, with no verb and no rule.

**Why it transfers:** it puts a person on the page instead of a list of do's and don'ts. Every gym already has the list. ASCEND's guide is one.

**Ground:** you need the staff member's permission, and a photo if they agree. Their name in type alone still works.

### C. Chess.com's Daily Puzzle becomes "find the crux"
**Source:** Chess.com Help Center, undated, opened 23 Sep 2026. → https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8708990-how-does-the-daily-puzzle-work

**The pattern:**
- One question, the same for everyone.
- Help comes in two stages: the first hint highlights the piece to move, the second shows the move.
- The discussion opens only after you have committed.

**Your version:** one problem photographed with its holds readable.
- **Slide 1:** the question.
- **Slide 2:** only the hold the setter says the problem turns on.
- **Slide 3:** the move.
- **Slide 4:** the setter's answer and where it is.
- **Ground:** you'll likely need to mark the holds on the photo, because same-colour holds are hard to read in a still.

**Hook:** a question whose answer already exists, with no number.

**Why it transfers:** a staged reveal turns a still into a puzzle without any motion.

**Refused, in part:** the hearts, lives and streaks. `medium does not carry`: they depend on an app tracking each person's attempts. Keep only the two-stage hint and the after-you-commit comments.

### D. A ski resort's snow report becomes a weekly set report
**Source:** Whitefish Mountain Resort snow report, no date printed, opened 23 Sep 2026. → http://skiwhitefish.com/snowreport/

**The pattern:**
- A fixed order every time: measurements first, taken at the same hour daily.
- Then a note signed by a named reporter.
- Then open/closed status by area.
- The page also says outright that the report "is a subjective assessment".

**Your version:**
- A weekly template with only the gym's own numbers, such as how many problems are on each wall.
- Then a note signed by that week's setter.
- Then status by wall: fresh, stripped, closed. Big Rock's September planner (Sep 2026) uses "resets" and "bouldering remains open" in the same way. → https://www.bigrockclimbing.com/september-setting-planner/
- Add a one-line caveat that grades are the setter's read.

**Why it transfers:** the same order every week teaches people where to look, and that is a template, which is your craft.

**Hook:** a measurement first.

### Refused: Strava "Year in Sport"
Source: https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401959-your-year-in-sport, opened 23 Sep 2026.
- **Reason:** `the reader cannot produce the ground`.
- The pattern is one personal statistic per card, built from each member's own recorded activity. I read that it needs at least three recorded activities in the year, an active subscription, and the app.
- Nothing survives without member-level data. I don't know what your gym can export.
- If it can, and the members agree, that is a separate conversation.

## 3. Also worth making, ranked

**2. Who's on the floor (2B).**
- **Why second:** it serves the neighbours who have never tried it, the half of your audience §1 doesn't reach.
- **Format:** four slides. The slide plan is in 2B.
- **Decide with the gym:** which staff member, which hours, and what the gym actually says in its first-timer welcome.
- **Fails if:** it becomes a list of rules.

**3. Find the crux (2C).**
- **Format:** four slides.
- **Decide with the gym:** which problem, and which single hold is the answer.
- **Fails if:** the first hint gives the answer away, or comments turn into people sparring over beta.

**4. Weekly set report (2D).**
- **Format:** a template of two or three slides, then the same every week.
- **Decide with the gym:** which numbers it truly counts, and the reset calendar.
- **Fails if:** it carries a number the gym can't stand behind.

## 4. What is moving

- **Boulderfest, first-timer framing.**
  - The comps run 25–26 Sep at chain gyms. Seattle's page answers "I've never climbed before, can I join? Yes!" and its bands are Recreational V0–V2, Intermediate V3–V5 and Advanced V6–V8. Its headline is "Built for all of us".
  - Youth aged 8+ is new for 2026. The Grand Finale is 23–24 Oct in St. Paul.
  - It's not your gym's event, but it is the message reaching your neighbours this week.
  - → https://boulderingproject.com/seattle-boulderfest/ and https://boulderingproject.com/brooklyn-boulderfest/, both undated pages
- **The training board as the community room.**
  - LEMUR (CBJ, 14 Sep 2026) is a vendor's claim: the board room is "one of the most photographed square metres in the gym", and Gen Z choose on "atmosphere, community".
  - Ripple Boulder, a comparable independent, lists a Tension Board 2 among its features (CBJ, 22 Apr 2026).
  - → https://climbingbusinessjournal.com/lemur-reborn-the-board-room-is-how-gyms-win-gen-z-and-lemur-is-rebuilding-its-brand-around-it
- **Retention over growth.**
  - CBJ (4 Sep 2026) tells operators to focus on existing customers and track "which first visits are turning into members".
  - → https://climbingbusinessjournal.com/6-ways-climbing-gyms-are-adapting-to-economic-downturns
- **"Same grade, different feel."** An ongoing argument, not this fortnight. Hörst (3 Dec 2025) and Boulder Movement (6 May 2025). → https://trainingforclimbing.com/climbing-gym-grade-variance-and-training-system/
- **Comp-style setting versus "pull and squeeze hard".**
  - Gripped (9 Jun 2026): two head setters describe parkour-style coordination problems.
  - Members at one gym resisted at first, and that gym ran a feedback whiteboard for eight months.
  - → https://gripped.com/indoor-climbing/climbing-gym-setters-talk-comp-style-boulder-problems/
- **The plateau.**
  - Central Rock Gym (1 Apr 2026) describes it as "You started out flying through the grades—V1, V2, V3—and then, one day, the progress just stopped."
  - → https://centralrockgym.com/blog/breaking-through-that-plateau/
- **Setting as authorship.** <creator>'s episode (11 Sep 2026), cited in 2A. Bouldering Project calls routesetting "athletic graffiti" on its main page (undated). → https://boulderingproject.com/climbing/
- **New holds and volumes.**
  - The CBJ grip report (Sep 2026) lists new families, rock-scan textures, dual-tex and a kids' family.
  - → https://climbingbusinessjournal.com/new-climbing-holds-and-volumes-september-2026
  - This is trade news. Whether your setters bought any is unknown.
- **What members complain about and ask for.**
  - The CWA survey (8 Apr 2026) lists peak-time crowding and unsupervised children as pain points, and penalty-free pauses and guest passes as most wanted.
  - → https://www.cwapro.org/blog/what-seven-years-of-climber-data-tell-us-about-where-indoor-climbing-is-headed
- **Designers told to avoid "AI-slop".** CWA (16 Mar 2026) has a marketing-ideas heading "Don't Rely on AI-Slop", and suggests a member T-shirt-design contest held during the event. → https://www.cwapro.org/blog/free-and-inexpensive-marketing-for-climbing-gyms
