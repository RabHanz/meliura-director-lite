## 1. Make this first: "The whole fly" (Partridge and Orange, exploded)

**Why this one first:**
- **Current:** it's the fly <creator> puts first in his "My Top 3 Spider Patterns", dated 16 Sep 2026.
- **Beginner objection:** the standing objection in beginner content is cost. The Essential Fly's kit guide (20 Apr 2026) says "Don't buy materials for every fly. Buy materials for the flies you actually want to tie." One frame can show the whole shopping list.
- **Makeable now:** the frame needs only what's already on your table. That's an inference, since I don't know what you own.
- **Swap:** if you don't tie this pattern, use the wet fly you tie with the fewest materials. The construction doesn't change.

**Format:** carousel of about nine slides, one per action.

**Cover:** the Partridge and Orange taken apart on plain white paper. The parts sit in one straight row at even gaps, in the order you use them: hook, thread, wire if you rib it, partridge feather. Nothing is tilted and nothing is labelled. The order is the order of use, not position on the hook, because every part ends up on the same shank.

**Slides:**
- **2:** the same photograph with each part named in the smallest legible type.
- **3–8:** one action per slide: thread on, wire on (skip if you don't rib), body, feather prepared and tied in, hackle wound, whip finish. The hackle slide gets the only arrow, showing the wrap direction. Each slide opens on the result of the last (§2, entry 2).
- **9:** the finished fly, on the same paper in the cover's spot.

**Hook:** a bare count and a flat claim, with the count taken from your own photograph. Count what's on the paper and don't take a number from me.

**Caption job:** ask which of the parts they don't own yet. That gives you their shopping list and tells you who's reading.

**What would make it fail:**
- Labelling the cover. The pattern only works if position carries the order.
- Scattering the parts in two directions. Both come from the exploded-drawing rules in entry 1.

**Pinterest:** I couldn't open it, so I have no evidence either way. The cover works as a standalone still.

## 2. It works in another niche. Here is your version.

**1. An architect's exploded drawing** → https://illustrarch.com/articles/architectural-diagrams/98403-exploded-diagram-architecture.html (page dated 14 Jul 2026)
- **Pattern:** every part is pulled apart along one axis, at even gaps, nothing rotated, each part lined up with where it sits in the whole. The page says leader lines go in "only if the assembly order is not already clear from position alone".
- **Your version:** the cover in §1.
- **Slides:** see §1.
- **Hook:** the source's line claims the layout tests the thing ("forces you to be honest about how a building actually goes together"). Yours claims a small count and lets the row prove it.
- **Why it transfers:** a materials list is an order, and position can carry an order.

**2. An origami diagramming guide** → https://langorigami.com/article/origami-diagramming-conventions/ (written 1989–91, online Aug 2000)
- **Pattern:** "Show one step per drawing." An arrow marks motion only. Every step's result is shown at once, so a mistake surfaces in the next frame and not five later.
- **Your version:** the step slides in §1. One action per slide, arrows only where something moves, and the next slide opens on the result. Compare the niche's own norm: flies-stepbystep runs 18 photos with bare step numbers as captions (§5). For turning the vise, the LEGO "rotate/flip" symbol is described at https://bricksfanz.com/a-guide-to-lego-instructions-symbols/ (9 Jan 2023).
- **Hook:** none of its own. It lives inside entry 1's carousel.
- **Why it transfers:** a beginner can't ask "did I do that right?", and showing the result each time answers it.

**3. A ceramicist's grid and line blend** → https://b2wceramics.com/ian-currie-grid-method/ (date unreliable, see notes) and https://help.glazy.org/testing/blending
- **Pattern:** change one thing in even steps along a row, hold everything else fixed and number the results. The page calls the finished grid "a map you can read at a glance". The Glazy example runs 0% to 10% in 1% steps.
- **Your version:** five Partridge and Oranges on the same hook and thread, with hackle turns rising from the fewest to the most, numbered by turns. The range and counts are your own. A second row (barbs stripped by half) is a later post; Global FlyFisher's hackle guide (20 Apr 1998) mentions stripping half the barbs on spiders.
- **Slides:**
  - **Cover:** the numbered row.
  - **2:** what changes along it, in your words.
  - **3:** which one you'd fish, and why.
  - **4:** tie your own row and hold one thing constant.
- **Hook:** the source opens on a count of results. Yours opens on how many flies and the fact that one thing changed. The count is the flies you tied.
- **Why it transfers:** the row shows one variable because everything else is held still. Hackle density is a live argument (Dark Skies, 13 Oct 2025, has a tip titled "How to Tie 'Sparse' Soft Hackle Flies").
- **Ground:** you'd need five flies photographed in one sitting, on paper.

**4. An entomology specimen label** → https://www.theskepticalmoth.com/techniques/labeling-your-specimens/ and https://mississippientomologicalmuseum.org.msstate.edu/Specimen_labels/Specimen_labels.html (updated 12 Oct 2021)
- **Pattern:** the specimen sits alone. A small stack of labels hangs below it: a first label "normally five lines long", with the name on a second label lower down. Observations such as weather and behaviour stay off the label and go in a separate journal.
- **Your version:** one fly on a plain ground. Under it a five-line recipe (hook, thread, body, rib, hackle) in the smallest legible type, and the pattern name on a lower label. The Partridge and Orange's recipe on Wikipedia is those five lines. Fishing claims stay in the caption.
- **Slides:** a single still, or a series with the same layout each time.
- **Hook:** none. The image is the hook.
- **Why it transfers:** a recipe is data about one object, and a label is where such data lives.

**Refused:**
- **Sourdough crumb shot** → https://foodshot.ai/blog/bread-photography (22 May 2026). `pattern inseparable from subject`. The cut face fills the frame because the interior is the evidence. A tied fly has no interior to show.
- **Hyper-realistic and oversized display-fly reels** → the "fly tying for beginners" Instagram tag page, which printed 1.1M and 44.4M for two of them (undated). `pattern inseparable from subject`. What travels there is the object's scale and realism, and there's no construction to carry. It also runs against the sparse dressing that the wet-fly pages describe (Sexyloops: "sparsely dressed").
