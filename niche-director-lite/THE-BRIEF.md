# What you hand back

One brief. Not a report, not a gallery, not a list of links with adjectives on them.

Write it for someone who will read the top of it once and go and make something. The six sections
come in this order, with the most usable first. The research notes go at the bottom, under the
briefs.

---

## 1. Make this first

One post, briefed completely, and one sentence on why this one comes before the others. Pick it.
Don't hand the choice back.

> **Make this first: "The mark that changes who did what."**
> **Why this one first:** the case-ending confusion is the live argument in the communities this
> fortnight (§4, line 2), and the construction for it (§2, first entry) needs nothing you don't
> already have: one mark, a flat ground, no photograph.
> **Format:** four-slide carousel.
> **Cover:** one harakah, cream ground, nothing else.
> **Slides:** 2, the word it changes and what it changes it to. 3, the same word with the wrong
> mark and what that would mean. 4, three words to try it on.
> **Hook:** a bare mark and no verb. The gloss waits until slide 2.
> **Caption job:** ask for the sentence they always get wrong. The threads suggest people have a
> specific one.
> **What would make it fail:** putting the gloss on the cover. The pattern depends on the mark
> staying unexplained for one beat.

The pick should be the post with the best evidence behind it that this creator can make this week
with what they have. Say which of those two things decided it if they pulled apart.

A good line names the *argument*, not the topic. "Broken plurals" is a topic. "People know the

## 5. Well made, and what we could establish about it

Up to eight references, each labelled **seen**, **described** or **counted** (`THE-METHOD.md` §3).
Each one has:

- **the URL you opened**, and the date you opened it
- **the director's note**: one or two sentences on how it is built, as construction, never as
  palette, marked "(from the maker's description)" when you did not see it
- **what to take, and what not to copy**
- **the reach basis**: read, compared, inferred or unknown, in those words

> **A bilingual grammar card on a cream ground, one rule per frame.** *(seen: your screenshot 3)*
> The card is one sentence of Arabic set at about half the frame width, the Latin gloss at about a
> fifth of it, and a photographed paper texture underneath at low contrast so the type sits on it
> without a box or an outline. Nothing else is in the frame. The size jump between the two scripts
> does all the hierarchy work. There is no colour doing it, no rule line, no icon.
> **Take:** the size ratio between the scripts, and the empty ground.
> **Do not copy:** the paper texture, which is the maker's own scan and half their signature.
> **Reach:** unknown. No count on the page, and I didn't find it cited anywhere.
> → https://… (opened 22 Sept)

Only references whose reach is **read** or **compared**, and low for this niche, go under the
heading **not yet travelled**. The rest go under **well made, reach unknown**.

**If you had no screenshots and found little described or counted, keep this section to two
lines**: what you looked for and what stopped you. A short section is honest. A padded one is not.

## Research notes

Below the briefs, because the creator reads them second. They tell the reader how much weight
the rest can take.

### What I could not reach

Plain and itemised, no apology.

> Pinterest's trend pages wanted an account. There are no save counts anywhere in this brief, so
> every Pinterest reference is reach unknown. Instagram post pages returned a login wall, so the
> three Instagram references come from search results and public roundups, and I did not see the
> posts themselves.

### What in here is inference

Every claim that is a reading rather than something you saw.

> That the audience is adult and mostly self-taught is an inference from how three community
> threads are written. I didn't find it stated anywhere.
> That the cream-ground grammar card is unusual in this niche is my reading against the roughly
> forty pages I opened, not against a measured distribution.

### Pages opened

The list, with dates. Short is fine. It lets the creator check any line above.

Then one line inviting a correction (see the skill, §6), and stop.

## The honesty rules

These are not style preferences. Each one is the difference between a brief and a plausible
document, and a plausible document is worse than nothing because someone will act on it.

1. **No invented number, ever.** No view count, save count, follower count, growth percentage,
   engagement rate or ratio that you did not read off a page you opened, and none put in the
   creator's mouth in a hook. If the method wants a number and you don't have one, write
   **unknown**. Unknown appearing several times means the brief is honest, not thin.

2. **Every reference is a page you opened.** Not a URL you constructed, not a plausible handle, not
   something you remember existing. If the fetch failed, the reference goes under *what I could
   not reach*. A fabricated link is the worst thing this can produce, because the reader will find
   it themselves and then trust nothing else in the brief.

3. **Label inference as inference**, inline and again in the notes. "This looks aimed at
   beginners" is a reading. "The bio says it is for beginners" is an observation. Both are useful.
   Mixing them up is not.

4. **Never describe a picture you did not see.** On a text-only host, craft comes from the
   creator's screenshots or from the page's own words, and the reference says which.

5. **Give the basis for every reach claim** in the four words: read, compared, inferred, unknown.

6. **Date everything**, including the day you opened it. A brief with no dates can't be checked
   next month, and one that can't be checked won't be trusted twice.

7. **Refuse rather than fill.** An empty section with a stated reason is a finding. A section
   filled with something adjacent is a lie with good manners.

8. **Never write the post.** Brief it. Once you draft the caption, the work has moved from the
   creator to the machine, and the audience can tell.
